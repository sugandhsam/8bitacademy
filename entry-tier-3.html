08.1

Model 5 · CISD

Tier 3 · Advanced ES + NQ 1m/3m NY am · session variants open ICT · structural

Why it works. CISD — change in the state of delivery — is the structural read of the same event Models 1–3 trade behaviorally. Price is delivered in one direction by a series of candles; when a level is swept and price then closes back through the opening price of that delivery series, the algorithmic delivery has objectively flipped. Everything is structural: a specific open, a specific close, a specific displacement. No thresholds, no ATR knobs, no statistics — the chart either printed the sequence or it didn't. That's also why it's last: it presumes fluency in levels, sweeps, and displacement.

CardRule
BiasReversal — against the old delivery, after the state change is proven
SetupA delivery leg (consecutive same-direction candles) runs into / sweeps a meaningful level (PDL, ONH, pool, wall tap). Mark the CISD line: the open of the first candle of that leg
TriggerA candle closes through the CISD line with displacement (an energetic body, typically leaving an FVG) — the state of delivery has changed
EntryOn the retrace into the displacement's origin — the FVG or the CISD line itself. Limit order, no chasing the displacement
StopBeyond the sweep extreme (the leg's terminal wick)
InvalidationRetrace closes back through the CISD line in the old direction → the "change" failed; full reset, wait for a new sequence
TargetOpposing liquidity: the pool the new delivery should draw toward (ONH/PDH for a long). 2–3R is the natural geometry
Management½ at +1R, BE; runner to the opposing pool. If the retrace never comes (price runs without you) — no trade, that's the model's cost of precision
Skip whenThe "close through" happens without displacement (grinding closes = no conviction) · the delivery leg swept nothing (a CISD in the middle of nowhere is noise) · major print inside the sequence
↯
With the tool (Tier 3): cisd marks delivery legs, CISD lines, and the qualifying close so the sequence is never mis-read live; fcz-ict adds the compression-zone context ICT entries key from; 8bf-open-lines supplies the ICT session opens (midnight / 08:30) that advanced variants anchor to. Everything stays structural — that's an academy invariant, not a preference.

Playing it with our levels

This is the model the whole stack was built for. The daily drop tells you which level's sweep matters: a delivery leg that terminates into a gexpro put wall or gamma wall tap and then prints CISD is the house A+ — structure and dealer mechanics agreeing on the same price. 8bfzonepro zones refine the entry: when the FVG overlaps a zone, the retrace entry sits at the zone's near edge. Session variants (Tier 3): the same sequence off the London sweep of Asia's range, or the 02:00–05:00 leg into the NY open — same rules, different liquidity map.

  • 1m ES: CISD off a put-wall tap from the daily drop, wall price annotated.
  • Counter-example: close through the CISD line with no displacement, grinding straight back — captioned "no displacement, no change".
Journal card · Model 5 — CISD
  • Delivery leg swept a real, pre-marked level (logged which one)
  • CISD line marked at the leg's first open before the trigger printed
  • Trigger close had displacement (FVG left behind) — grinding closes skipped
  • Entry was a resting limit at the FVG / line — never chased the displacement
  • Stop beyond the sweep extreme, never widened
  • Missed retrace = no trade, logged as a valid no-fill rep
08.2

Progression & journal protocol

Advancement runs on evidence of rule-compliance, never on P&L. A green week of broken rules is worse than a red week of perfect execution — one is luck, the other is a trader being built. This mirrors how real capital is won: allocators buy process logs, not screenshots of one good day.

The rep

One rep = one journaled trade or valid no-trade (a setup that completed but was correctly skipped, e.g. CISD with no retrace). Each rep records: date · instrument · model · screenshot at entry time · the model's journal card ticked · outcome in R · self-grade.

GradeDefinitionCounts toward compliance?
AEvery card item ticked, executed exactly per planyes
BValid setup, minor execution slip (late entry, early partial) — notedyes
CAny rule broken: no setup, widened stop, size change, revenge tradeno

The gate

Per model: 20 reps minimum · (A+B)/total ≥ 80% · at least 12 A-grades · all models of the tier gated before moving up. Exception: Model 0 gates at 10 reps — its card is fully binary, so there is nothing to judge, only to obey. Screenshots are mandatory — a rep without a chart is a story, not evidence. P&L is recorded but plays no role in the gate. Tier 2 graduation is also where 8BF script access is granted.

◎
Why 20 and 80: twenty reps won't prove an edge — that's not their job. They prove the student can see the setup and obey the model under fire, which is the only thing the next tier builds on. Edge gets proven later, with the same journal, at 100+ reps. Start the habit now.

Grading integrity

Self-graded, spot-checked. Random reps get pulled for review; a C self-graded as A is worth a conversation, a pattern of them ends the ladder. The journal is the student's future track record — teaching them to falsify it would be teaching the one unrecoverable habit.

08.4

Glossary

R
One unit of planned risk — entry to stop. All results are journaled in R, not dollars.
Trigger candle
Model 0's unit: one candle at a marked line that touches it, closes back, and engulfs the prior body — defining the whole trade at its close.
OCO bracket
One-cancels-other order set: entry + stop + target placed together. Filling the target cancels the stop and vice versa — management without decisions.
OR / ORH / ORL
Opening range (09:30–09:45 ET) and its extremes.
PDH / PDL · ONH / ONL
Prior day RTH high/low · overnight (Globex) high/low.
Liquidity pool
A price where stops cluster — beyond obvious highs/lows and equal extremes.
Sweep / raid
A push through a pool that takes its stops. Becomes a raid (tradeable) only if price fails back.
Reclaim
The close back on the original side of a swept level — proof the break was a liquidity event.
Acceptance
Two consecutive closes beyond a level — the level has flipped roles. The universal invalidation.
Displacement
An energetic, directional candle sequence that leaves imbalance (FVG) behind — conviction made visible.
FVG
Fair value gap — the unfilled gap a displacement leaves between candle 1's extreme and candle 3's.
CISD
Change in the state of delivery — a close through the open of the delivery leg that made the extreme.
Delivery leg
The consecutive same-direction candle series that ran price into a level.
Regime
The higher-timeframe answer to "trend or rotation?" — gates Model 4.
Gamma wall / put wall / call wall / HVL
Dealer-hedging levels from the gexpro greek extraction — where mechanical flows defend or accelerate price.
Confluence
Two independent level sources within ticks of each other. The quality multiplier.
Rep
One journaled trade or valid no-trade with screenshot, card, and grade. The unit of progression.
08.5

Self-check

If any answer surprises you, reread that chapter before the next session.

A perfect Model 0 trigger candle closes, you place the bracket — but the next candle never touches your entry. What do you do, and what goes in the journal?
Cancel the order at that candle's close. It's a valid no-trade rep — journal it with the screenshot and grade it like any other. Chasing the move that leaves without you is the C-grade the model exists to train out.
Price touches PDH for the third time today. First Touch trade?
No. The model is first touch — a third test means the level's resting orders are spent and it's being eaten. If anything, the map is hinting at acceptance and a breakout.
09:52, price pokes above ORH and prints two full closes above it. Your OR Raid short trigger?
There isn't one — two consecutive closes outside is acceptance, the breakout case. The model stands down. No trade is a valid rep; journal it.
PDL is swept. Four bars later price is still below the level. Entry?
No. The reclaim window is 3 bars. A late crawl back is not a reclaim — the pool was accepted through, and the second sweep that often follows would be aimed at you.
Model 4 setup looks perfect on the 1m, but 5m VWAP is flat and price has crossed it five times. What now?
The regime gate failed — there is no Model 4 today, no matter how pretty the 1m looks. Rotation days belong to Models 1–3.
A candle closes through your CISD line, but it's a small grinding body with no FVG. State changed?
Not tradeably. The close must come with displacement — no energy, no conviction, no entry. Skip it and log the sequence.
You're +2R on the day after 2 trades, and a mediocre B-setup appears. The skeleton says?
You have one trade left of three — but the skeleton never obliges you to use it. Marginal setups after a good day are how green days end red. The journal rewards the skip.
Week 3, you're down −4R overall but 17 of 19 reps graded A/B. Are you failing?
You're succeeding at the only thing the gate measures: execution under fire. Variance owns 20-rep P&L; you own compliance. Finish the reps, then evaluate the model — not yourself — with the data.
