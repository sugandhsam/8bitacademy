Entry Models · Tier 1 · Beginner

The first three rungs

Model 0 plus the first two entry models for ES & NQ scalping — 1m/3m execution, NY RTH. Naked chart, hand-drawn levels, no indicators. Every model here stands alone on public levels.

06.1

How the ladder works

⚠
Codified playbook, not a backtest: these models are codified from live discretionary trading, not from a measured sample. Frequency and reliability statements are priors. The progression protocol in Tier 3 is what turns them into evidence — every student builds their own sample before sizing up. Trade micros (MES/MNQ) until your journal says otherwise.

This is a ladder, not a menu. The models are ordered so each rung teaches exactly one new concept and reuses everything below it: bracket mechanics → levels → the open → liquidity → trend flow → delivery. You trade one model at a time until your journal proves you execute it by rule — then the next rung unlocks.

◎
The one-line doctrine: an entry model is a permission system, not a prediction system. It tells you the only moments you are allowed to act — everything outside those moments is flat. Mastery = the model fires and you execute it identically every time.

Every model chapter has the same anatomy: why it works → model card (the rules) → schematic → with the tool → playing it with our levels → journal card. The model card is the law; everything else is commentary.

06.2

The risk skeleton

Uniform for all five models, all tiers, non-negotiable. Models define where the stop and target live; the skeleton defines how much and how often. Scalpers don't blow up on entries — they blow up on the fifth unplanned trade.

06.3

Level literacy

All five models are statements about behavior at a level. The models don't care where the level came from — a level is a level. What changes with the source is quality, and quality is what confluence measures.

Level classes — public (draw these by hand, every day)

LevelWhat it isBehavior bias
PDH / PDLPrior day RTH high / lowPrime liquidity pools — stops rest beyond them
Prior settleYesterday's settlementMagnet on rotational days
ONH / ONLOvernight (Globex) high / lowFirst raid targets after the open
ORH / ORLOpening range extremes (09:30–09:45)The day's first auction brackets — Model 2 lives here
VWAPSession volume-weighted averageThe fair-price rubber band; trend days ride one side of it
Gap levelsPrior close vs today's openOpen gaps want filling on rotation days

Level classes — ours (8BF engines)

SourceWhat it givesHow students get it
Daily level dropgexpro chart string + 3-line ramp read, posted pre-openEveryone, Tier 1 up — draw the lines by hand
gexpro wallsGamma wall, call/put walls, HVL from UW greek exposure — ES-nativeLevels via the drop; script at Tier 2
8bfzonepro zonesAuto supply/demand zones with defined depth — a level with a bodyScript at Tier 2

The three reads that matter

1 · Fresh vs tested. A level's first touch of the session carries the most unfilled orders. Every retest spends them. First touch = fade candidate; third touch = the level is being eaten.

2 · Reaction vs acceptance. A wick through a level that closes back = reaction (the level held). Two consecutive closes beyond it = acceptance (the level flipped roles). Every model's invalidation is some form of acceptance.

3 · Confluence. Two independent level classes within a few ticks (PDH + gamma wall; ONL + zone edge) multiply quality. One-source levels are B-setups; confluent levels are A-setups. Grade before entry, not after.

06.4

Model 0 · Trigger Candle

Tier 1 · Rung Zero MES / MNQ 3m only 09:30–11:30 ET zero discretion

Why it exists. Every model above this one asks for a judgment call — "is the approach stretched?", "is that a real regime?". Model 0 asks for none. It is the entry model that removes entry-model thinking: one binary candle pattern at a pre-marked line, and the moment that candle closes, the entire trade — entry price, stop price, target price — is defined. The student's only job is the loop: mark the line → wait → check the pattern → place one bracket order → hands off → journal. That loop, not this model's P&L, is what every later rung is built on.

⚠
Read this before trading it: Model 0's edge is thin by design. Fixed targets ignore structure and the pattern fires on mediocre lines too. It is a mechanics trainer, not a strategy — its output is a student who can execute and journal flawlessly, on micros, for 10 reps. Then it retires and Model 1 begins.
CardRule
BiasWhichever side of the line the trigger candle closes on — the candle decides, not you
SetupA pre-marked line: any level from the daily drop, or hand-drawn PDH/PDL/ONH/ONL. Marked before the session, never mid-move
TriggerOne 3m candle that does all three: ① touches the line ② closes back on your side of it ③ its body engulfs the previous candle's body. Three checks, each yes/no. Any "no" = no trade
EntryStop-order 1 tick beyond the trigger candle's high (long) / low (short), placed as an OCO bracket with stop and target attached
Stop1 tick beyond the trigger candle's opposite extreme
InvalidationNext candle closes without filling the entry → cancel the order. No fill = valid no-trade rep
TargetFixed +1.5R — computed from entry−stop distance, placed with the bracket
ManagementNone. No partials, no trailing, no moving anything. The bracket finishes the trade; touching it after entry is an automatic C-grade
Skip whenTrigger candle is taller than the previous 3 candles combined (stop too far) · within 2 min of a scheduled print · daily trade limits reached
↯
With the tool: there is none, on purpose — and there never will be for this rung. Model 0 on a naked chart is the point: if a student can't run the loop with two horizontal lines and one order ticket, an indicator won't fix that.

Playing it with our levels

The daily drop is the ideal line source: it hands beginners 3–5 pre-marked prices with zero drawing skill required, and gamma-wall lines give the pattern its best hit rate. Rule for Rung Zero: take the drop's levels as given, don't rank them yet — level quality judgment is Model 1's curriculum, not this one's.

  • Candle closed back on my side — yes
  • Body engulfed the previous body — yes

06.5

Model 1 · First Touch

Tier 1 · Beginner ES + NQ 3m trigger · 1m refine 09:30–11:30 ET any level class

Why it works. A fresh level is where resting orders concentrate — traders who marked it yesterday, dealers defending strikes, stops clustered just beyond. The first time price arrives, those orders are all still there. The model does one thing: sells the first test of a fresh level after a stretched approach, and only once the chart proves rejection. It is the purest level-literacy trade, which is why it's rung one.

CardRule
BiasCounter — fade back toward the mean (VWAP / first opposing level)
SetupPrice makes a stretched approach (several consecutive candles, no meaningful pullback) into a level that is untouched this session
TriggerTouch bar wicks through / tags the level but fails to close beyond it, then the next bar (confirmation) closes back past the touch bar's midpoint
EntryClose of the confirmation bar (market), or stop-order at its extreme for better price honesty
StopBeyond the touch-bar wick extreme + a small buffer (a few ticks — spread, not opinion)
InvalidationAcceptance: two consecutive 3m closes beyond the level before you're filled → stand down. No flip trades at Tier 1
TargetFirst opposing level or VWAP, whichever is nearer. Natural geometry ≈ 1.5R; if the math gives < 1R, skip
ManagementSkeleton default: ½ at +1R, stop → BE, runner to target
Skip whenLevel already tested today · approach is a relentless one-way drive off a catalyst (news trend) · level sits < 1R from VWAP (no room)
↯
With the tool (Tier 2+): no signal painter for this model — that's deliberate. Tier 2 adds 8bfzonepro, which turns the line into a zone with depth: trigger at the near edge, stop beyond the far edge, and the zone's fresh/tested state read off the chart instead of your memory.

Playing it with our levels

The daily drop upgrades this model the most: a gamma wall first touch is the single highest-quality fade in the catalog — dealers are mechanically hedging against you crossing it. Priority order when several levels are fresh: gamma wall > call/put wall confluent with PDH/PDL > 8bfzonepro zone edge > naked PDH/PDL > ONH/ONL. On positive-gamma days from the ramp read, first-touch fades are the day's bread; on negative-gamma days, demand extra confirmation or stand down — walls break on those days.

  • 3m NQ: stretched approach into fresh PDH, wick rejection + confirmation bar. Mark level, entry, stop, target, R multiple.
  • 3m ES: gamma-wall first touch from the daily drop — same annotations + the drop text pasted in a corner.
  • Counter-example: third test of the same level slicing through — captioned "spent level, no trade".
Journal card · Model 1 — First Touch
  • Level was untouched this session (fresh) — verified before entry
  • Approach was stretched (no meaningful pullback into the level)
  • Touch bar failed to close beyond the level
  • Entered only on/after the confirmation bar — no front-running the touch
  • Stop set beyond the wick extreme before entry, never widened
  • Target ≥ 1.5R at entry time; skipped if < 1R of room
06.6

Model 2 · OR Raid

Tier 1 · Beginner → Inter NQ primary 1m/3m 09:45–11:00 ET opening range

Why it works. The first 15 minutes build the day's opening bracket, and breakout traders immediately lean on its extremes — with stops just beyond. The first push through an OR extreme is as often a stop-raid as a breakout. This model doesn't guess which: it waits for the push to fail back inside, then trades the trapped traders' unwind toward the other side of the range. Fires nearly every session on NQ, which is exactly what a learning sample needs.

CardRule
BiasCounter to the raid — back into and across the opening range
SetupOR = 09:30–09:45 high/low, drawn at 09:45 sharp. After it's set, price pushes through one extreme
TriggerThe push fails to hold: a 1m/3m bar closes back inside the range after trading beyond the extreme
EntryClose of the re-entry bar back inside the OR
StopBeyond the raid extreme (the poke's wick) + buffer
InvalidationAcceptance outside: two consecutive closes beyond the extreme, or a successful retest of the level from outside → it's a breakout, not a raid. Stand down
TargetT1 = OR midpoint · T2 = opposite OR extreme. Skip if OR is so narrow that T1 < 1R
Management½ at T1, stop → BE, runner to T2. Runner dies at 11:00 if neither target nor stop is hit
Skip whenGap-and-go trend day (open drives one way on news — raids don't exist) · OR > ~1.5× recent average (targets too far) · first 5 min after a 10:00 data print
↯
With the tool (Tier 2+): orb-raid draws the OR automatically and marks raid vs breakout states, so the acceptance judgment isn't eyeballed. Until then: two horizontal lines at 09:45 and your own eyes — the model is fully tradeable naked, which is why it's a Tier 1 rung.

Playing it with our levels

Raid quality jumps when the OR extreme is confluent with an outside level: an OR high sitting just under ONH, PDH, or a gexpro wall means the poke sweeps two pools before failing — more trapped traders, cleaner unwind. Best variant: the raid extreme tags a gamma wall from the daily drop and rejects — that's a First Touch and an OR Raid in one candle, and it's an automatic A-grade. If the ramp read calls a trend day (negative gamma, one-sided), skip raids entirely: acceptance is the base case on those days.

  • 1m NQ: raid extreme confluent with ONH — annotate both levels and the double sweep.
  • Counter-example: genuine breakout (2 closes outside + successful retest) — captioned "acceptance, stand down".
Journal card · Model 2 — OR Raid
  • OR drawn at 09:45 sharp, from 09:30–09:45 extremes only
  • Waited for a close back inside — did not fade the poke itself
  • No entry after two consecutive closes outside the range (acceptance)
  • T1 was ≥ 1R at entry — skipped narrow ORs
  • Stop beyond the raid wick, set before entry, never widened
  • Runner closed by 11:00 regardless of feelings
